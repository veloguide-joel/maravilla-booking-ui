Bucerías Guide

Bucerías is a beach town on Banderas Bay, in the Riviera Nayarit, a short drive north of Puerto Vallarta. It is big enough to have everything you need and small enough to walk. This is a short guide from people who live here.

The beach

The town runs along one long, wide sandy beach on the bay. The water is generally calm, which makes it good for swimming, paddleboarding and long walks, and sunsets over the bay are the daily event. In spring the afternoon wind picks up and the bay fills with kitesurfers.

The town

Bucerías has two halves, divided by a dry riverbed. The centro has the plaza, the church and the open-air market stalls. Across the footbridge is the Zona Dorada, the "Golden Zone", with galleries, cafés and restaurants along and around Avenida Lázaro Cárdenas. In high season the galleries hold an evening art walk once a week.

When to come

  • November to April is the dry season: sunny, warm days and cooler evenings. It is the busiest time of year.
  • December to March is whale season. Humpback whales come into Banderas Bay and can often be seen from shore or on a boat trip.
  • January brings the town's patron saint festival, with processions, music and fireworks around the plaza.
  • June to October is hot, green and humid, with afternoon and evening rain. It is quieter and prices are lower.

Getting here and around

  • By air: fly into Puerto Vallarta (PVR). Bucerías is roughly half an hour north of the airport by road, depending on traffic.
  • In town: most things are a walk away. Taxis are easy to find, and local buses run along the highway to the neighbouring towns and to Puerto Vallarta.
  • Time zone: Bucerías keeps the same time as Puerto Vallarta.

Nearby

  • La Cruz de Huanacaxtle, the next town north, has a marina and a popular Sunday market in season.
  • Nuevo Vallarta, just south, has a marina, golf and a long resort beach.
  • Punta de Mita and Sayulita, further up the coast, are good day trips for surfing and a change of scene.
  • Puerto Vallarta has the Malecón, the old town and the widest choice of restaurants and nightlife.

Good to know

  • The currency is the Mexican peso. Cards are widely accepted, but small shops, taxis and market stalls prefer cash.
  • Drink bottled or purified water.
